Overview

Animal Playground is a browser-based musical toy designed for young children with minimal reading skills. It allows children to move animal characters around the interface to explore different sounds and create simple musical combinations through direct interaction. The project aims to make sound exploration accessible without requiring children to understand traditional musical notation. It responds to the project's purpose by allowing children to experiment with sound through simple movement and interaction, while supporting its worth through curiosity, creativity and playful exploration.

Area 1 — Spatial Interaction

My first area of exploration is Spatial Interaction, as movement and position are important parts of how children interact with the musical toy. Since the project is designed for young children with minimal reading skills, I am interested in how space itself can be used to guide interaction without relying on written instructions or musical knowledge.

For this area, I am particularly interested in the balance between structure and freedom. Too much structure may limit exploration, while a more open space may create more opportunities for children to experiment and discover unexpected sounds. This relates to the project's focus on curiosity, and how spatial interaction can encourage children to learn through moving, trying and discovering.

1.1 Five Lines Music player

The five-line design is the original interaction concept for the project. It uses a familiar and structured arrangement inspired by musical lines, but simplifies the interaction so children do not need to understand traditional musical notation.

1.2 Colour Sound Zones

The idea of Colour Sound Zones is to connect children’s curiosity with sound exploration. Inspired by colourful children’s xylophones, where different colours represent different notes, this prototype replaces the fixed music lines with coloured sound areas. Children can freely move animals between these areas to discover different sounds, creating a more open and exploratory spatial interaction without relying on traditional musical notation.

1.3 Sound Zones Random

The idea of Random Sound Zones extends the previous Colour Sound Zones prototype by making the interaction more playful and unpredictable. Since the previous prototypes use fixed layouts, I introduced randomisation so that each refresh creates a new arrangement of sound zones. This gives children a different space to explore each time, encouraging them to experiment with new positions and discover different sound combinations.

Area 2 - Feedback

My second area of exploration is Feedback, as it can shape how children experience and respond to the musical toy. For young children, the response they receive from an interaction can become part of the play itself, rather than simply confirming that an action has worked. I am interested in exploring how feedback can make each interaction feel more rewarding and encourage children to continue experimenting. A more responsive experience may create a stronger sense of involvement, where children become interested in seeing and hearing what happens next. This makes feedback important not only for usability, but also for creating a playful experience that can hold their attention over time.

2.1 Five-line Scaleup

While developing the interaction, I noticed that sound alone didn't clearly indicate which animal was playing, making it hard to follow the sequence. To clarify this connection, this prototype introduces a scale-up animation when each animal plays. The movement draws attention to the active animal and creates a clearer connection between the visual and sound. It also adds more visual activity to the playback, making the experience feel more responsive and playful.

2.2 Note Range Feedback

After exploring visual feedback, I also wanted to investigate how changes in sound itself could become part of the feedback While developing the project, I wanted to give children more musical choices, as the fixed note range limited the variety of melodies they could create. I added a button to switch between higher and lower notes, allowing children to explore how pitch can change the sound of the same arrangement. This also introduces a simple musical concept through playful interaction.

2.3 Overlap Feedback

For this prototype, I focused on how feedback could encourage children’s curiosity. I explored whether the random overlapping colour zones could create unexpected and playful responses. Since animals are already the main characters of the interaction, I decided to use animal sounds as feedback when overlap occurs, creating a small surprise for children to discover through exploration.
